    How the compression works

    Each image is decoded by your browser, resized if you chose a maximum width, and saved again as JPG, WebP or PNG. Nothing is sent to a server, so it works with private documents and even offline once the page has loaded.

    Hitting an exact size such as 50 KB

    In Target size mode the tool searches for the highest quality that still fits under your limit. It tries a middle quality, checks the file size, then moves up or down, halving the gap each time, until it is within about half a percent. If even low quality is too big, it reduces the dimensions step by step until the file fits, so the result always stays under the limit you set.

    Quality mode

    Choose a quality from 10% to 100%. Around 70% to 80% is usually indistinguishable from the original for photos and makes them 60% to 90% smaller.

    Privacy bonus: location data removed

    Photos from phones often contain hidden EXIF data, including the GPS location where they were taken. Re-saving through the browser strips this metadata, which makes compressed copies safer to share. Orientation is applied first, so photos stay the right way up.

    JPG, PNG or WebP?

    • JPG is best for photos and is accepted everywhere. It does not support transparency, so transparent areas become white.
    • WebP is usually 25% to 35% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality and keeps transparency. All modern browsers support it, but some older upload forms still reject it.
    • PNG is lossless: perfect for screenshots, logos and text, but large for photos. Re-saving a PNG as PNG only gets smaller if you also reduce its dimensions, which is why Automatic saves PNGs as WebP, keeping any transparency.

    Tips for the smallest files

    • Resize first. A 4000-pixel phone photo shown 1200 pixels wide wastes over 90% of its pixels. Reducing dimensions saves more than lowering quality.
    • Crop before compressing so you do not spend bytes on background.
    • Avoid compressing the same file again and again. Each JPG save loses a little detail, so start from the original.
    • Use the before/after slider to check faces and text before you submit.

    Privacy

    Images are processed with your browser's own image functions. They are not uploaded, logged or stored, and they disappear when you close the page. Only your settings, such as the target size, are remembered on this device.

    Image compression questions

    How do I compress an image to 50 KB?

    Choose Target size, press 50 KB, then drop in your photo. The tool finds the highest quality that fits under 50 KB and shrinks the dimensions if needed. Download the result when it shows a size under 50 KB.

    Are my images uploaded to a server?

    No. Compression happens inside your browser. Your photos never leave your device, which also means it works with private documents.

    Does compressing reduce quality?

    Slightly, but at 70% to 85% quality most people cannot see a difference in photos. Use the before/after slider to check. Reducing the dimensions often saves more space with less visible change.

    Can I compress many images at once?

    Yes, up to 50 at a time. Each one can be downloaded separately, or all together as a single ZIP file.

    Why did my PNG not get smaller?

    PNG is a lossless format, so re-saving it at the same size changes little. Convert it to WebP or JPG, or reduce its maximum width, to make it much smaller.

    Does it remove location data from photos?

    Yes. Re-saving through the browser drops EXIF metadata such as GPS location, camera model and date, while keeping the photo the right way up.

    Can I compress HEIC photos from an iPhone?

    Safari can open HEIC files, so on an iPhone or Mac it works there. Other browsers cannot read HEIC yet: set your iPhone camera to Most Compatible, or share the photo as JPG first.
